## Dimensions

### architecture — 7.5 (lane-core)
- Four-plane split (loop / core / app_server / TUI) enforced by 17 ADRs and a real backend-contract suite (`tests/app_server/backend_contract/`, 17 files instantiating either backend); the loop is separated from every delivery surface through typed events and a session-backend port (`_session_backend_port.py`) — crush's fused `agent.go` failure is absent.
- Docked from cline's 8 by the residual: `_unified_harness_backend_adapter.py` 9,519 LOC re-implementing session semantics a second time, `AgentLoop` 3,966 LOC / 142 methods / 30-kwarg ctor explicitly noqa'd (`_loop.py:528`), `VibeApp` 6,669 LOC (`app.py:673`) — the errata >5k product-surface docking factor.
- Integrator note: the CI gap on the unified leg (c1/s7) is priced **only** in verification; the architecture dock here is the structural facts themselves (dual-stack existence + god files), not the missing workflow.

### verification — 7.5 (lane-safety, between cline's 8 and crush's 7)
- 651 test files / ~209.7k LOC; the CI pytest run carries the whole permission corpus, including escalation-pair property tests against both shell backends (`test_bash.py:3446-3545`, incl. `cat $FILE` vs `cat notes > /etc/cron.d/pwn` at :3532) and unified-harness resolver contracts (31 tests) — this is why 7.5 rather than crush-adjacent 7.
- Docked: ubuntu-only matrix with retry-masking (`ci.yml:97-104`); the 367-scenario PTY golden suite and all Rust tests run in **no** workflow (Makefile-only, `pyproject.toml:304-305`); GrowthBook can roll users onto the unified backend (`_experimental_harness.py:15`) whose Python seam never runs in CI (c1, the study's headline dual-backend risk); no fuzzing, no evals (errata ceiling keeps verification below 9 regardless).

### safety-enforcement — 6 (lane-safety, top of the pi/cline/crush rung)
- Ceiling: approval prompt, unconditionally waivable. Zero OS machinery repo-wide (grep for bwrap/seatbelt/seccomp/landlock/RLIMIT empty in both backends); `--yolo` short-circuits even the denylist before `resolve_permission` (`_loop.py:2943-2948`), unlike grok-build's floored yolo. Rungs turn on the existence of an enforcement layer beneath the prompt, and there is none — so top of the 6 rung, not the 8 side.
- Above the ceiling the policy layer is the strongest non-codex one in the study: tree-sitter bash analysis failing closed on unreadable syntax (`_shell_permission_analysis.py:287-303`), per-binary option guardrails (`_shell_command_policy.py:312,576`), an anti-widening grant algebra with literal flags and encoded path grants (`permissions.py:44-58,71-134` — rationale docstrings state the exact escape class), deny-or-prompt on every uncertainty path (`_request_broker.py:49`, `base.py:144`, `layer.py:149,314`).
- Counted holes: partial, spoofable injection tagging — highest-volume untrusted channels (bash stdout, web_fetch, MCP results) enter untagged with no tag-forgery screening (s5); no egress approval beyond per-origin grants, no SSRF screening (s4); honesty minimal-not-misleading (SECURITY.md disclosure-only; s8).

### token-economy — 7 (lane-econ, cline rung)

## Score disagreements resolved

The three lanes scored disjoint dimensions, so there are no numeric conflicts to arbitrate; five qualitative tensions are resolved explicitly:

1. **"Weakest dimension" claim collision.** Lane-core's weakest was dual-backend verification, lane-safety's was the enforcement ceiling, lane-econ's was token-economy. Resolution: on the ten-dimension board the weakest is **safety-enforcement (6)** — the CI gap is already priced into verification 7.5 and the trigger one-sidedness into token-economy 7; neither is a lower rung than safety's no-layer-beneath-the-prompt ceiling.
2. **c1 vs s7 overlap (CI-absence cited twice).** Both findings cite "no cargo test in any workflow." They stay as two records — different subjects of the claim: c1 is the *dual-stack migration* risk (a remote rollout can enable a backend whose seam CI never exercises), s7 is the *un-gated high-fidelity suites* (PTY goldens, Rust). Cross-refs recorded both ways; the fact is docked once, in verification.
3. **r-core used the CI gap as "decisive for both scores."** Resolution: architecture 7.5 is kept, but on re-inspection its dock is restated as structural (9,519-LOC second stack + three god files); testability of the seam is verification's evidence, not a second architecture dock. No number changes; the reasoning is now consistent.
4. **econ said interop "one rung above cline" yet scored 8, and cline's interop is 8.** Resolution: 8 stands. Protocol breadth (ACP, binaries, rival import) genuinely exceeds cline's MCP+surfaces, but cline's 8 already banks breadth elsewhere; rungs, not adjectives, score. vibe@8 ≠ codex@9: no MCP server, no published SDK.
5. **Half-point scores (7.5 x3).** No lane conflict (dimensions disjoint); half-points are deliberate between-rung placements documented per dimension above. Weighted total computed with the half-steps: 73.5. Sensitivity: if c6's memory-only checkpoint log turns out to be compensated in the Rust store (c6 is the one med-confidence dock), operability → 8 and the total becomes 74.0 — band unchanged.

## Verdict

**Strongest: interop (8).** A complete ACP server with fork/resume/list/session-mode (`acp/agent.py:452-1127`), five-platform prebuilt `vibe-acp` binaries shipped through the published Zed extension, headless json/streaming on the same app-server as the TUI, an MCP client with per-server sampling — plus the corpus-unique rival-plugin import layer (`DetectedPluginFormat` over codex/claude-code/kimi/opencode/agent-plugins.org with ambiguity handling and path-traversal guards, re-verified first-hand). No other subject in the study imports its rivals' ecosystems as a first-class input; nothing here reaches codex's 9 because the protocol stays internal (no MCP server, no SDK).

**Weakest: safety-enforcement (6).** The most property-defended approval layer of any non-codex subject — fail-closed tree-sitter shell gating, anti-widening grant algebra, deny-or-prompt on every uncertainty path — sitting on a ceiling of *approval alone*: zero OS sandbox in either backend, and `--yolo` short-circuits even the denylist before policy resolution (`_loop.py:2943-2948`), with the highest-volume untrusted channels (bash output, web fetches, MCP results) entering the transcript untagged and un-screensaved (s4, s5). Top of the "tested approval, nothing underneath" rung, exactly where pi/cline live — without pi's SECURITY.md candor about the boundary (s8).
