## Adjudication 1: sync-fork vs divergent-fork vs honest-rebrand vs original

**Divergent fork with honest attribution-preserving rebrand.** Evidence:
- Not original: manifest identity still `codex-monorepo` (package.json), 4,072 files hash-identical to codex at census, README.md:47 declares "Open Interpreter is a fork of OpenAI's Codex", LICENSE/NOTICE byte-identical to the codex snapshot (re-verified `diff -q` clean).
- Not a sync-fork in the codex-infinity sense: no upstream-sync automation anywhere (`scripts/`, workflows, justfile contain no upstream-merge machinery; the `upstream` grep hits in workflows are inherited codex release text). Instead the fork pins a tagged baseline "rust-v0.154.0 compatibility baseline" (RELEASE_NOTES.md:3-4) and ships its own 0.0.x release line with fork-authored release notes -- periodic manual rebase, not daily merge.
- The delta is architectural, not a patch series: ~30k own Rust LOC, 6 own crates, a transport-route enum extension, a tool-registry seam, a scheduler, a session-import crate, an ACP server. A divergent fork can outrank upstream on merit per protocol; moot here -- 83.0 < codex 88.5 either way. (If the dispatcher insists on the census rename-clone label, rule (a) caps at <88.5 and this total still complies.)
- Honest-rebrand sub-verdict stands: FORK_BRANDING.md:1-25 is an explicit anti-deception branding policy ("Do not globally replace codex"; identity centralized in `product-info` and tested via `cli/tests/product_identity.rs`); SECURITY.md routes upstream bugs to OpenAI's VDP.
